# Trademark policy

Centina and Unreason3D are released under the [MIT License](LICENSE). MIT is a
**copyright** license: it grants broad rights over the code and says nothing at
all about names. That silence is where confusion usually starts, so this page
says the part MIT does not.

## Reserved

**Unreason3D**, **Centina**, **SuperB**, **UnWire**, **SuperBridge** and
**Charybdis** are names of this project and its parts. Please do not use them as
the name of your own product, or in any way that implies endorsement by or
affiliation with this project.

## Expressly released

**The command vocabulary is free for anyone to use** — `Loft`, `Sweep2`,
`Revolve`, `Flatten`, `Split`, `Blend` and the rest of it.

Unreason3D exists to teach, and a central part of that is a vocabulary a
student carries *between* applications — the words are meant to be the same
ones they will meet in Rhino, in Fusion, in whatever they use next. A shared
teaching grammar wants to spread. Fencing it would defeat the point of having
built it.

## Permitted

You may state truthfully that your product is **built on Centina**, or that it
**uses Centina**. That is nominative use.

What is not permitted is naming your product Centina, or presenting it in a way
that suggests this project endorses it.

## If you are unsure

Ask. The intent here is to keep names from becoming confusing, not to police
anybody's work.
